\begin{abstract}
We study contextual Write--Read--Value (W/R/V) attention in approximately 98.2M-parameter causal language models. W/R/V uses eight contextual read heads, two shared contextual write/value heads, per-head RMS normalization, rotary position encoding, and causal softmax through PyTorch scaled dot-product attention. In a controlled H200 comparison, GQA and W/R/V use the same tokenizer, FineWeb-Edu stream, 100,007,936-token budget, optimizer, peak learning rate, BF16 numerical mode, evaluation protocol, and three seeds. W/R/V improves held-out loss on all three paired seeds: its mean is $4.684432\pm0.021137$ versus $4.703035\pm0.017657$ for GQA, with a mean paired difference of $-0.018604$. Mean training throughput is 121,448 versus 125,021 tokens/s, a 2.86\% penalty. With only three pairs, the 95\% $t$ interval for the mean loss difference, $[-0.0541,0.0169]$, includes zero; we therefore report a consistent small-scale observation rather than universal superiority. Historical fixed-state WVR runs and lexical-write variants are retained only as architecture-path evidence. The controlled W/R/V condition disables the lexical write residual, isolating the contextual attention path used in the main comparison.
\end{abstract}
